Project operating system

Eight gates from
lead to care.

No gate is bureaucracy for its own sake. Each prevents a common, expensive failure.

  1. Qualify.

    Confirm problem, fit, decision-maker, budget reality, content owner and deadline before detailed work.

  2. Contract and fund.

    Signed agreement, agreed scope and cleared deposit before capacity is reserved.

  3. Kick off.

    Confirm objectives, roles, source materials, access, communication channel, meeting cadence and approval dates.

  4. Structure.

    Approve sitemap, page purposes, content responsibilities and functional requirements before visual polish.

  5. Design.

    Present decisions in context. Collect consolidated feedback from the nominated approver within the agreed rounds.

  6. Build and verify.

    Implement, test responsive behaviour, accessibility, content, integrations, performance and error states.

  7. Launch gate.

    Payment condition, approvals, backups, analytics, domains, redirects, forms and rollback plan are all confirmed.

  8. Handover and care.

    Transfer ownership, train, document, define support boundaries and schedule the first post-launch review.

Scope control

A change is not
a moral failure.

New information can improve a project. The professional response is to make the consequence visible before doing the work.

Included clarification

Same outcome, agreed boundary

Correcting implementation against the approved scope, resolving your defect, or using an agreed revision round to refine the approved direction.

Change request

New outcome, asset or complexity

New pages, new features, new audience, new integration, changed approved direction, extra revision round, delayed content that compresses the schedule, or work created by a third-party system.

Useful line

“Yes, that can be added. It sits outside the current scope, so I’ll document the cost and schedule impact before we decide whether to include it now or put it in phase two.”

Solo weekly rhythm

Protect delivery
and pipeline.

Do not let paid work consume every hour and then act surprised when the pipeline is empty.

Every morning / 30–45 min

Sales movement

Prospecting and follow-up before reactive messages take the day. Record every next action.

Client delivery

Protect two focused production blocks, with communication batched around them.

Friday / 2 hours

Launch gate

No heroic
Friday launches.

Schedule normal-hours launch windows with time to observe and reverse.

Before

Ready to switch

  • Final approval captured
  • Payment condition met
  • Production backup and rollback plan
  • DNS access and current records saved
  • Redirect map verified
  • Forms tested to real recipients
  • Legal and privacy content approved by client
During

Controlled change

  • Record exact switch time
  • Verify TLS and canonical domain
  • Test critical pages and forms
  • Check analytics and consent behaviour
  • Watch logs and uptime
  • Communicate status once facts are known
After

Close properly

  • Mobile and desktop smoke test
  • Search indexing and sitemap checks
  • 404 and redirect monitoring
  • Client training and ownership record
  • 14–30 day review booked
  • Testimonial and case study permission requested

Security and ownership

Never become the
single secret.

Clients should own critical accounts, and you should have the access needed to operate—not permanent possession of their business.

Accounts

Client-owned by default

Domains, paid platforms, analytics, payment services and core hosting should be registered to the client with you invited using an individual account.

Credentials

Password manager + MFA

Never email reusable passwords in plain text. Use role-based access, multi-factor authentication and a documented offboarding process.

Recovery

Backups must be restorable

Define backup frequency, retention, location and ownership. A backup you have never tested is only a theory.

Capacity rule

Do fewer things
at once.

Context switching feels busy and hides delay. Limit work in progress.
