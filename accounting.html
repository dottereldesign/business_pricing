Accounting is not a mysterious annual ritual. It is a small weekly habit: capture every transaction, reconcile it, reserve money that is not yours, and know what remains.

Simple answer

If you launch as a sole trader and want tax handled for you, Hnry is the low-admin path. If you want more control, become a company, add payroll, or need deeper reporting, use Xero with an accountant or bookkeeper. Either way, separate your accounts and keep source documents.

The four-account system

Give every dollar
a job.

These can be fee-free sub-accounts if your bank supports them. The separation matters more than the branding.

01 / Income

All client money lands here.

No personal groceries. No mystery subscriptions. This becomes the clean record of business inflows.

02 / Tax

Reserve before spending.

Move a planning percentage from each payment. Start conservatively, then replace the percentage with an adviser-backed estimate.

03 / GST

Only if registered.

The GST portion of a GST-inclusive receipt is 3/23. Hold GST separately, then pay the net amount after allowable input claims.

04 / Operating

Tools and bills leave here.

Transfer a deliberate owner draw to your personal account. Do not treat the income balance as disposable cash.

Planning rule

While you are learning your real tax position, reserving roughly 25–30% of net profit for income tax and ACC is safer than guessing low. GST is separate. This is a buffer, not a tax calculation; use the Tax page and professional advice.

Bookkeeping rhythm

Tiny routines.
Clean year.

The system works because each task has a home and a frequency.

Every invoice

Make the sale traceable.

  • Unique invoice number
  • Your legal/trading name and contact
  • Client legal name and address
  • Issue date and due date
  • Clear milestone or service description
  • Amount and GST treatment
  • Bank account and payment reference
Every week / 30 min

Close the gaps.

  • Match bank transactions
  • Upload or attach receipts
  • Send overdue reminders
  • Move tax and GST reserves
  • Update lead and project cash forecast
  • Check next 30 days of bills
Every month / 60 min

Read the business.

  • Revenue, expenses and profit
  • Unpaid invoices and days overdue
  • Cash runway
  • Revenue by service and client
  • Software cost per active client
  • Hours sold versus hours used
Every quarter

Prepare before due dates.

  • Review GST return if registered
  • Compare tax reserve with forecast liability
  • Review provisional tax dates if applicable
  • Cancel unused software
  • Check upcoming annual renewals
  • Update prices if margin or workload is wrong
Every year

Close and learn.

  • Complete IR3 as a sole trader or company returns as required
  • Provide records to the accountant/tax service
  • Review fixed assets and depreciation treatment
  • File company annual return if incorporated
  • Review ACC classification and invoice
  • Set next-year budget and revenue target

What to record

Useful categories,
not a junk drawer.

Consistency makes tax and management reports useful. Ask your adviser before inventing a complicated chart of accounts.

Income

Separate revenue types.

  • Website projects
  • UI/development services
  • Hosting and care
  • Design and collateral
  • Digital products or licensing
  • Reimbursed costs
Normal operating costs

Track the real burn.

  • Software and subscriptions
  • Hosting and domains
  • Advertising and sponsorship
  • Accounting and legal
  • Insurance
  • Bank and payment fees
  • Training and professional memberships
Needs judgement

Do not assume 100% deductible.

  • Home office
  • Phone and internet mixed use
  • Vehicle and travel
  • Meals and entertainment
  • Equipment and depreciation
  • Clothing
  • Personal subscriptions with some business use

Keep evidence and ask.

IRD record keeping

Seven years.
Readable. Backed up.

A bank transaction is not always enough to prove what was purchased and why it was business-related.

Keep

Invoices, receipts, taxable supply information, credit notes, bank and card records, contracts, asset purchase details, calculations, filed returns and evidence supporting any business/private allocation.

If you are already behind

Do not hide
from the pile.

Late records get more expensive and less accurate. Start with completeness, then categorise.

  1. Download all business bank and card transactions.

    Mark the date range and identify every account used.

  2. Gather invoices and receipts.

    Search inboxes by vendor, export billing histories and photograph paper records.

  3. List missing evidence and unclear transactions.

    Do not quietly invent a category.

  4. Reconcile month by month.

    Start at the oldest open period and keep a corrections log.

  5. Bring in a professional before filing guesses.

    A clean-up fee is cheaper than compounding errors, penalties and anxiety.
