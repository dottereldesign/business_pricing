Decision one

Sole trader
or company?

Both can invoice under a trading name. The difference is the legal and administrative structure underneath.

Working recommendation

Launch as a sole trader, get a free NZBN, use separate accounts, carry appropriate insurance and revisit a company after stable revenue, higher-risk contracts, hiring, a business partner or advice specific to your situation.

02 / Inland Revenue

Tell IR you are self-employed.

  • Use your individual IRD number
  • Confirm income sources in myIR
  • Understand schedular payments if a payer uses them
  • Register GST only when required or strategically chosen
  • Record income and expenses from day one

03 / NZBN

Apply for the free NZBN.

It is optional for a sole trader but recommended. You will need the IRD number used by the business, identity evidence, RealMe and a MyNZBN account.

With a NZ passport or driver licence, many sole trader applications are processed instantly.

If you choose a company

More structure.
More duties.

Registering is not the finish line. A company needs ongoing administration and directors must understand their obligations.

  1. Create RealMe and a Companies Register account.

    Use the official Companies Office site, not a lookalike registration service.

  2. Reserve the company name.

    The current online fee is NZ$10 plus GST. Once approved, you have 20 working days to complete incorporation.

  3. Prepare director, shareholder and address details.

    Understand what information becomes public and use appropriate service/address arrangements lawfully.

  4. Incorporate and return consent forms.

    The current incorporation total is NZ$136.55 including GST. The company automatically receives an NZBN.

  5. Set up IR, banking and accounting for the company.

    Do not casually mix company and personal spending. Agree how you will be paid with an accountant.

  6. File the annual return.

    It confirms company details and is not a tax return. Missing it risks removal from the register.

GST decision

Watch the
$60k line.

The test is turnover from taxable activities, not profit or money left after expenses.

You must register

Past or expected threshold

IRD says you must register if taxable turnover was over NZ$60,000 in the last 12 months or is expected to go over NZ$60,000 in the next 12 months.

Once registered, charge GST correctly, keep taxable supply information, file returns and pay the GST collected after allowable claims.

You may register voluntarily

Below the threshold

Voluntary registration can make sense when clients are mostly GST-registered businesses and setup purchases are meaningful. It also creates filing work and changes how consumer-facing prices feel.

Do not add “plus GST” to invoices unless you are registered.

Easy-to-miss obligations

Small does not
mean exempt.

You are the person responsible for these until you appoint someone else.

Privacy

Name a privacy officer.

The Privacy Act requires NZ organisations, including small businesses and sole traders, to have a privacy officer. In a solo studio, that will normally be you. Publish a privacy statement and collect only what you need.

Marketing email

Consent, identity, unsubscribe.

Commercial electronic messages with a NZ link need appropriate consent, accurate sender identification and a functional unsubscribe. A single message can be spam; cold outreach needs careful relevance and lawful consent grounds.

Claims and pricing

Be accurate and provable.

The Fair Trading Act prohibits misleading claims about price, features, deliverables and performance. Avoid fake discounts, invented capacity or promises of guaranteed results.
