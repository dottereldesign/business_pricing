The recommendation

Start as a focused solo studio selling websites and improvement work to small New Zealand organisations. Operate lean, use your own name while testing, and do not spend months inventing a brand before you have a sales habit.

The first 90 days

Build evidence,
not theatre.

Each phase has one job. Do not advance because the calendar says so; advance because the outputs exist.

Days 01–30 / Foundation

Make it buyable.

  • Choose sole trader or get advice on a company
  • Open separate bank accounts
  • Choose one core website offer
  • Finish pricing, proposal and agreement
  • Create a list of 50 realistic prospects
  • Ask five people for blunt offer feedback
Days 31–60 / Conversations

Make it known.

  • Contact five well-chosen prospects each weekday
  • Publish one useful teardown or case study weekly
  • Run discovery calls with a written script
  • Send proposals within 24 hours
  • Track lead source, stage and next action
  • Win a small, controlled first engagement
Days 61–90 / Proof

Make it repeatable.

  • Deliver with weekly client updates
  • Protect scope and collect payment on schedule
  • Ask for a testimonial and referral
  • Turn delivery into a case study
  • Review margin, time and client fit
  • Raise or narrow the offer if demand supports it

Six decisions that matter

Decide enough
to move.

Most early decisions are reversible. Treat them like working hypotheses, not identity.

01 / Structure

Sole trader first is reasonable.

It is the lightest NZ structure for one person testing demand. A company becomes more compelling when liability, hiring, partners, retained profit or larger contracts justify the admin.

Compare structures →
02 / Position

Sell the result, keep the range.

Lead with “websites and digital improvement for NZ organisations.” You can still deliver UI, CMS, React tools, integrations, collateral and care underneath that promise.

Build the offer stack →
03 / Name

Your own name is a valid launch name.

It transfers existing portfolio trust and avoids losing weeks. A studio name can come later if you want the business to feel larger or become saleable.

Explore 3,000 options →
04 / Price

Scope before discount.

Your $3,500 starting point is plausible, but only if the scope is controlled. If the prospect cannot afford it, shrink the deliverable rather than quietly donating weeks.

Review pricing →
05 / Marketing

Outbound before paid ads.

At this stage you need conversations and positioning data. Search ads can scale a working funnel; they cannot rescue an unclear offer or an empty proof library.

Use the channel plan →
06 / Money

Separate it on day one.

Dedicated income, tax and operating accounts make every later decision easier. Treat tax and GST as money you are holding, not money you earned.

The honest bit

Skill is not
the bottleneck.

Your portfolio already proves you can build. The next skill is consistently asking the right people whether they need help—and tolerating silence without disappearing.

Hard truth

A better logo, another framework and ten more internal pages will not create a business by themselves. Every week needs a non-negotiable block for outreach, follow-up and asking for the sale.

Do this this week

Seven moves.
Then repeat.

If everything feels too large, only do these in order.

  1. Pick the launch structure.

    Read Setup, then confirm with an accountant if your risk or personal situation is unusual.

  2. Open separate accounts.

    Income lands in one place; tax and GST are moved immediately.

  3. Choose one entry offer.

    Recommended: a focused website or website reset with controlled scope.

  4. Prepare the sales minimum.

    One-page scope, agreement, invoice template and discovery questions.

  5. Build a list of 25 prospects.

    Choose businesses with a visible problem you can explain specifically.

  6. Send five useful messages.

    No bulk spam. One observation, one possible consequence, one easy next step.

  7. Book the next block.

    Put tomorrow’s outreach time in the calendar before polishing anything else.
