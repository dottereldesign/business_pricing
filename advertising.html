Recommendation

For the first 90 days: 60% direct prospecting and follow-up, 20% referrals and partnerships, 15% proof content, 5% small channel tests. Do not run broad Meta ads for “web design” until you know who converts and why.

Organic-first 90 days

One market.
One useful signal.

Choose a prospect group where your commercial proof makes sense: schools and education suppliers, established local services, membership bodies, or organisations with content-heavy CMS sites.

Weeks 01–04 / Learn

Talk before targeting.

  • Pick one segment for the month
  • Build 50 researched prospects
  • Interview five friendly contacts
  • Send five tailored messages each weekday
  • Publish one teardown each week
  • Log objections word for word
Weeks 05–08 / Prove

Show the thinking.

  • Publish a focused case study
  • Create one useful checklist or calculator
  • Ask past colleagues and clients for introductions
  • Partner with a copywriter, photographer or IT provider
  • Attend one relevant local event
  • Repeat the message that earns replies
Weeks 09–12 / Test

Buy a small signal.

  • Choose one paid channel only
  • Use a dedicated landing page
  • Set a hard spend cap
  • Track qualified lead—not click—cost
  • Follow up within one business day
  • Stop, change or scale from evidence

Paid funnel calculator

Pay for leads,

Use qualified leads, not form fills. Revenue is expected value; real results arrive as whole projects and can be much lumpier.

Start with a fixed loss limit. If the landing page and follow-up are untested, assume your first campaign is buying information.

NZ guardrails

Do not market
like a pest.

Useful targeting and honest claims are both commercial strategy and compliance.

Electronic outreach

Consent still matters.

NZ commercial electronic messages require an appropriate consent basis, accurate identification and a clear, functional unsubscribe. A conspicuously published business address can create deemed consent only where the message is relevant to the person’s business role and no no-contact statement appears.

Advertising claims

Have a reasonable basis.

The Fair Trading Act prohibits misleading representations and unsubstantiated claims. Do not promise rankings, revenue, “limited spots” or savings you cannot demonstrate.
