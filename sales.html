● Sales / A repeatable conversation

Find the fit.
Ask for the work.

Good sales is not tricking someone into a website. It is diagnosing an expensive problem, proving you can handle it, defining a safe next step, and being willing to say no.

touches

Five tailored prospecting or follow-up actions each weekday.

Not bulk messages. Not 25 hours. Consistent, researched movement.
Core rule

Every active lead needs a stage, a next action, an owner—you—and a date. “Waiting to hear back” is not a stage unless a follow-up date is attached.

The seven-stage pipeline

Move it or
close it.

A spreadsheet is enough at first: company, person, source, problem, value, stage, next action, next date and notes.

  1. Prospect identified.

    A specific organisation, buyer and visible reason to contact them.

  2. Contacted.

    A relevant message was sent lawfully; first follow-up date is scheduled.

  3. Conversation.

    They replied or were introduced. Confirm whether the problem is real before pitching.

  4. Qualified discovery.

    Problem, impact, budget reality, decision-maker, timing and constraints are understood.

  5. Proposal sent.

    Scope, exclusions, price, payment, schedule and expiry are explicit. Follow-up is already booked.

  6. Won / deposit paid.

    The signed agreement and cleared deposit—not enthusiasm—reserve capacity.

  7. Lost / nurture / no fit.

    Record why. Close it cleanly, schedule a genuinely relevant future check-in, or stop.

Discovery call

Diagnose before
you prescribe.

Thirty minutes is often enough for a first call. Your job is to understand the business case, not run a free design workshop.

Problem

Why now?

  • What triggered this conversation?
  • What is the current site failing to do?
  • Who experiences the problem?
  • What have you already tried?
Impact

What changes if it works?

  • What action should visitors take?
  • How is that outcome measured?
  • What is delay costing?
  • What does a strong result look like?
Reality

Can this move?

  • Who approves scope and money?
  • Is a budget range allocated?
  • What deadline is real—and why?
  • Who supplies and approves content?
Fit

Say back the problem.

“What I’m hearing is [problem], it matters because [impact], and you need [outcome] by [date]. Have I got that right?” If they disagree, keep listening. If they agree, recommend the smallest sensible engagement.

Next step

Do not end vaguely.

Agree one of three outcomes: proposal by a named time, a paid discovery phase, or no fit. If another stakeholder is required, invite them before producing a detailed proposal.

Proposal anatomy

Make yes safe.
Make scope visible.

A beautiful 30-page deck is optional. Clear commercial terms are not.

The document

What the proposal must answer

  • Situation: what you understood about the business and problem
  • Outcome: the job the project should do
  • Scope: pages, templates, features, content responsibilities and integrations
  • Process: stages, decision points and client inputs
  • Schedule: assumptions, dependencies and target dates
  • Investment: total, GST treatment, deposit, milestones and validity
  • Boundaries: exclusions, revision rounds, change process and third-party costs
  • Acceptance: agreement, invoice and start condition
Payment structure

Fund the work.

Smaller projects: consider 50% to book, 50% before launch/handoff.

Larger projects: consider 40% to book, 30% after approved design, 30% before launch/handoff.

Your final terms need to match NZ law, the contract and project risk. Do not transfer final assets or launch without the agreed payment condition.

Conversation starters

Human messages.
No guru voice.

Personalise the observation and only contact people where you have a lawful, relevant basis. These are structures, not bulk templates.

Warm introduction ask

Ask one person, specifically.

Hey [Name] — I’ve started taking on focused website and front-end projects directly. I’m especially useful for organisations with a content-heavy or outdated site that is hard to manage. If someone in your network mentions that kind of problem, would you be comfortable introducing us? No pressure to sell me—an intro is enough. Here’s the work so you know what you’d be sharing: [portfolio link]
Relevant cold outreach

Observation, consequence, invitation.

Hi [Name] — I was looking at [specific page] while researching [sector]. On mobile, [specific issue] makes [important action] difficult to find. I build and improve content-heavy websites, and I sketched three practical fixes I’d prioritise. Happy to send the short note if useful—no pitch deck required. Jamie Wilson [identity/contact] If you would rather not hear from me again, reply “no thanks” and I’ll close the loop.
Proposal follow-up

Make the decision easier.

Hi [Name] — checking in on the [project] proposal I sent [day]. The key decision is whether [outcome] is important enough to address by [timeframe]. Is there a question, missing stakeholder or scope concern stopping a decision? I can adjust the route if the objective remains the same. If timing has changed, that is useful to know too.
Close the loop

Stop chasing forever.

Hi [Name] — I haven’t heard back, so I’m going to close this enquiry for now rather than fill your inbox. If [problem] becomes a priority later, reply here and I can confirm current capacity. Either way, thanks for the conversation.

Red flags

A bad client
is expensive.

Early revenue pressure makes every enquiry look precious. Protect your time and reputation.

Commercial

Money fog

  • Refuses a deposit
  • Will not name a decision-maker
  • Wants a quote before explaining scope
  • Promises exposure or future work instead of payment
  • Asks you to hide costs from stakeholders
Delivery

Impossible inputs

  • Fixed launch date but no content owner
  • Unlimited decision-makers
  • “Make it pop” is the entire brief
  • Wants copying of a competitor
  • Declines accessibility, testing or security basics
Behaviour

Trust the pattern

  • Disrespectful before money changes hands
  • Repeatedly misses meetings without warning
  • Pushes you to start before agreement
  • Rewrites history after calls
  • Creates false emergencies
Walk away line

“I do not think I’m the right fit for the way this project needs to run, so I’m going to decline rather than promise a process I cannot support.” You do not need to prosecute the whole case.
