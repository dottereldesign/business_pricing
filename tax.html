Four systems to understand

They are related.
They are not the same.

Most confusion comes from calling every government payment “tax.”

01 / Income tax

Tax on taxable profit/income

Self-employed people use their individual IRD number, generally report net profit in an IR3, and can claim eligible business expenses with records.

  • NZ tax year is normally 1 April to 31 March
  • IR3 is generally due 7 July unless a tax agent or extension applies
  • Other income can change the total and marginal rate
  • First year is not tax-free
02 / GST

Tax collected on taxable supplies

Registration is required if turnover was over NZ$60,000 in the last 12 months or is expected to exceed it in the next 12 months. Voluntary registration is possible below that.

  • Standard GST rate is 15%
  • GST portion of an inclusive price is 3/23
  • Claim eligible input GST with evidence
  • GST collected is not revenue
03 / Provisional tax

Next year’s income tax paid during the year

IRD says provisional tax generally applies when residual income tax from the previous return is more than NZ$5,000. It spreads tax into instalments rather than one lump sum.

  • Due dates depend on method, balance date and GST frequency
  • Late surprises hurt cash flow
  • Voluntary early payments may be possible
  • Tax pooling may help in some situations—ask an adviser
04 / ACC levies

Separate ACC invoice

Self-employed levies depend on liable income, business Classification Unit and cover type. ACC says self-employed people generally pay three levies and are invoiced after income is reported to IRD.

  • Check the Business Industry Classification code
  • Default cover is generally CoverPlus unless another cover is chosen
  • Do not use a generic percentage as a final estimate
  • Use ACC’s official levy estimator

Cash-flow trap

A great month can
create a bad bill.

Tax arrives later than the work. Without reserves, success can make the bank balance lie.

Do this

When an invoice is paid, move GST immediately if registered and move an income-tax/ACC reserve based on your forecast. Reconcile it quarterly. Never finance ordinary spending with money set aside for government obligations.

Calendar

Track your dates
in myIR.

Exact payment dates vary with balance date, filing frequency, method and extensions. Use myIR as the source of truth.

  1. 31 March — common end of income year.

    Close records and confirm outstanding items.

  2. 7 July — common IR3 filing deadline.

    Applies unless you have a tax agent or an extension of time.

  3. GST dates — based on your filing frequency.

    Monthly, two-monthly or six-monthly settings create different due dates.

  4. Provisional tax dates — based on your method.

    Do not copy dates from a blog; check the obligations shown in myIR.

  5. ACC invoice — budget separately.

    Check MyACC and the classification used after filing.
