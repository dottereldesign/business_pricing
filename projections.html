Capacity reality

Revenue has a
delivery cost.

At $3,500 per project, NZ$100k in project revenue means about 29 projects. That is more than two full websites every month before sales, admin, support and time off.

Lever one

Raise average value

Add high-value discovery, content structure, collateral, integrations and improvement plans when they solve the client’s job.

Lever two

Reduce delivery variance

Reusable checklists, starter components and defined content inputs improve margin without making every site look the same.

Lever three

Add careful recurring revenue

Care smooths the floor, but $50/month alone needs many clients. Improvement retainers can carry more value if responsibility and capacity are explicit.

Forecast rules

Keep the model
honest.

Update monthly with actuals, not confidence.

  1. Separate booked, likely and aspirational revenue.

    A proposal is not cash; a verbal yes is not a deposit.

  2. Model payment timing.

    Deposits and milestones matter more to cash flow than annual invoice totals.

  3. Include non-billable time.

    Sales, admin, learning, sick days and holidays are real capacity.

  4. Use a downside case.

    Ask what happens if leads stop growing, one invoice is 30 days late, or a project overruns.

  5. Do not call revenue take-home.

    Subtract direct costs, overhead, tax and ACC; then preserve working capital.
